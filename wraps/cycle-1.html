menstrual · apr 26–may 2
estrogen and progesterone both at their lowest
both hormones have bottomed out, which triggers the lining to shed. the prostaglandins released during this process are what caused your cramps — intense on day 2 when they peak, then easing off. exhaustion on days 3 and 5 is estrogen still very low. but confident showing up on days 4 and 5 is the first sign estrogen is beginning its climb. your body turning a corner before the bleeding even stops.
follicular · may 3–~may 14
estrogen rising — this is your window
estrogen climbs steadily through follicular phase, and your symptom log reflects it perfectly. high energy logged 6 times between may 4–12. confident twice. right side of the bed on may 4. this is what rising estrogen feels like: clear, capable, outward-facing. estrogen supports serotonin and dopamine, which is why this phase tends to feel the most like yourself. it peaked somewhere around may 13–14 just before ovulation.
ovulation · ~may 14–15
estrogen peaks, then a brief dip — progesterone begins rising
estrogen spikes just before ovulation, triggering the LH surge that releases the egg. after that, estrogen dips briefly and progesterone begins climbing. this transition is often where things shift. for you, may 15 is exactly where the turn happened: turned-off and bad skin. that bad skin specifically is a classic early progesterone signal — it can trigger changes in sebum production as progesterone rises and estrogen simultaneously dips.
luteal · may 15–21
progesterone dominates, then both drop — the weight before the wave
progesterone is calming but also flattening — it can suppress the serotonin lift that estrogen was providing. that's where the low energy and turned-off feeling comes from. then in the final days, both estrogen and progesterone fall sharply. that double withdrawal is what late luteal really is. anxious, brain fog, discomfort, low energy on may 16–21 — your body processing the drop. "calmly violent" on may 21 is late-luteal estrogen withdrawal. it has a name and it's real.
spotting · may 20–21
progesterone dropping — the lining starts to shift
pre-period spotting is often linked to progesterone falling below the threshold needed to maintain the uterine lining. the brown spotting on may 21 — minimal, with cramps that never arrived — is textbook late-luteal progesterone withdrawal. the red spotting on may 20 was likely a small bleed as the lining started shifting. your period followed 2 days later, exactly on schedule.
